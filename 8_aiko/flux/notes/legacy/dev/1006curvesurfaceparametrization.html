1006CurveSurfaceParametrization

1006 ◉ Curve and Surface Parametrization

[!info]- Revision history

Rev Date Author Note
V1 2015-02-28 AV

Curves

Line3

A line is defined in its own coordinate system, and stretches along the X axis in that system. (Technically, the orientations of the Y and Z axes are irrelevant, so we store only the Origin and X-vector rather than storing a complete coordinate system). The parametrization is in mm, and is simply the distance along the X axis.

Arc3

An arc is defined in its own local coordinate system, where it lies in the XY plane, and winds around the Z axis. The center of the arc is at the origin, and T=0 corresponds to a point lying on the X axis. T is measured in radians.

Definition

R = radius of the arc

Ellipse3

The parametrization of Ellipse3 is identical to that of an arc. The ellipse is defined in the XY plane, with center at the origin. T is measured in radians, winding clockwise about the X axis

Definition

XRadius = radius of the arc measured along the X-axis YRadius = radius of the arc measured along the Y axis

Note that the X and Y directions must be the major and minor axes of the ellipse, though either of them can be the major, and the other one the minor. That is, XRadius may be smaller or larger than YRadius.

ConicCurve3

The parametrization of Conic Curve varies, depending on the Form of the curve. This type can be used to represent ellipses, hyperbolas or parabolas (all the conic sections) and the parametrization is different for each type. For an exhaustive definition, see the IGES reference, Entity type 104.

This is rarely used, except to import type 104 entities from IGES. It is possible that in the future we may deprecate this, and convert these entities into NurbCurve on import.

PiecewiseCubic3

This represents a piecewise cubic curve; there are multiple knots in the curve, and between each knot the curve is a cubic with its own cubic polynomial coefficients. The parametrization can be understood by studying the polynomial curve (Entity type 112) in the IGES reference.

Like ConicCurve3, this may be deprecated in the future, and we will convert PiecewiseCubic3 into NurbCurve on import.

Polyline3

This represents a simple piecewise linear approximation, given by N points (N >= 2). The parametrization goes from 0 .. N-1. The integer part of the parameter is used to select a segment within the polyline, and the fractional part is the lie along that segment (0=start of segment, 1=end of segment). Thus, a point with parameter 2.5 lies at the mid-point 3rd segment in the Polyline3.

NurbCurve3

This is a classic NURBS curve, and the parametrization is given by the knot vector.

Surfaces

Cylinder

A cylinder is defined in its own local coordinate space and is then lofted into the desired location/orientation using a transform.

Definition

R is the radius of the cylinder in mm.

Parametrization

U goes around the Z axis, along the XY plane, and is in radians (0...2*PI) V goes along the Z axis, and is in mm (unbounded)

Cone

A cone is defined in its own local coordinate space and has a transform to move it into the desired location/orientation.

Definition

R is the radius of the cone at the XY plane. As we go higher in Z, the radius increases. A is the semi-angle subtended by the cone at the tip. At a given Z, radius = R + tan (A)

Parametrization

U goes around the Z axis, along the XY plane and is in radians (0 .. 2*PI) V goes vertically along the Z axis and is in mm

Plane

A plane is defined in its own local coordinate system. In this coordinate system, the plane coincides with the XY plane.

Parametrization

U is in mm along the X axis of the local coordinate system (-Infinity .. +Infinity) V is in mm along the Y axis of the local coordinate system (-Infinity .. +Infinity)

RuledSurface

A RuledSurface is defined by two generator curves, and we obtain the surface by connecting corresponding points of these two curves with straight lines. The two generator curves must therefore be of equal parametric domains.

Definition

Cb is the bottom generator curve. Ct is the top generator curve (which must have the same parameter domain as Cb)

Parametrization

V is used to evaluate points Pb on curve Cb and Pt on curve Ct using this as parameter input to these parametric curves. V must lie between Tmin .. Tmax of the domain of Cb. U is used to interpolate between Pb and Pt and must lie between 0 .. 1

Sphere

A sphere is defined in its own local coordinate system.

Definition

R is the radius of the sphere in mm

Parametrization

U goes around the Z axis, along the XY plane, and is in radians (0 .. 2*PI). V goes vertically and is the elevation angle of the point from the XY plane. The domain is -PI .. +PI

SpunSurface

A SpunSurface is defined by rotating a generator curve about the Z axis.

Definition

Cg = the parametric generator curve that is spun around the Z axis

Parametrization

V is used to evaluate a point Peval on the generator curve Cg. The domain of V is the same as the T domain of Cg. U is used to rotate the point Peval around the Z axis in a counter-clockwise direction, and is measured in radians. The domain of U is 0 .. 2 *PI

Note: The picture shows a generator curve that seems to lie in the XZ plane. This is not mandatory and is not always the case; the generator curve may be positioned anywhere in space, and need not be planar. The point we get when evaluating at U=0 is not necessarily going to lie in the XZ plane; no matter where that point is, it is just rotated about the Z axis by U radians to get the final point.

SweptSurface

A SweptSurface is defined by moving a generator curve Cg along a direction D

Definition

Cg = the parametric generator curve that is swept. Dir = a unit vector defining the sweep direction

Parametrization

U is used to evaluate a point Peval on the generator curve Cg. The domain of U is the same as the T domain of Cg. V is used as a distance to move Peval along the direction vector Dir. V is measured in mm, and the domain of V is -Infinity .. +Infinity.

Torus

A torus is defined in its local coordinate system and is lofted up into space with a transformation. A torus is defined by taking a small circle with center on the X axis, and lying in the XZ plane and sweeping it around the Z axis.

Definition

RMajor = radius of the big circle we get when move along the centerline of the torus tube RMinor = radius of the small circle that is rotated around to form the torus

Parametrization

U is the rotation of the generator circle about the Z axis; U = 0 corresponds to the generator circle lying in the XY plane. U goes from 0 .. 2*PI. V is the angle around the generator circle itself. At U=0, V=0 corresponds to a point lying at RMajor+RMinor (the furthest point) and as V increases, we go around the generator circle counter-clockwise.
