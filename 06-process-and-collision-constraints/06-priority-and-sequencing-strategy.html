Priority and Sequencing Strategy

Priority and Sequencing Strategy

[Target]

Scope

The rest of this chapter constrains where piles may be placed (reachability, orientation, collision). This chapter is about when/in what order piles are built and unloaded — a scheduling concern layered on top of the spatial placement produced by 05-stack-and-pile-management/03-auto-arrange.md. It merges three related use cases: priority-based stack building, sort sequence for process safety/productivity, and multi-part-pick optimization.

Priority-based stack building

Downstream processes should not wait for a pallet to be entirely finished before starting on it. Building piles in a priority order — completing the piles a downstream process needs first, earliest — lets a pallet become available to downstream sooner, reducing its idle time and smoothing overall material flow. This requires an explicit pile-priority signal (e.g. derived from the downstream process's own consumption order) that Auto Arrange and the proposal engine in 05-auto-suggested-stacking-patterns.md can take as an additional objective, alongside area/order-grouping.

Sort sequence for process safety and productivity

The order in which parts are removed from the sheet (not just where they end up stacked) affects two things:

  1. Sheet stability — removing structurally difficult/large parts too early can let the remaining skeleton shift, endangering later picks from the same sheet. Difficult parts should generally be sequenced later in the unload order, once the sheet is otherwise cleared and stable.
  2. Gripper economy — grouping picks that share a gripper/orientation reduces gripper changes and pick retries; sequencing that scatters gripper-incompatible parts forces avoidable tool changes.

Both are expressed through the pile's Members ordering (Stacking no. per 05-stack-and-pile-management/01-stack-data-model.md), which is what ultimately becomes Unload Sequence in the exported LST (08-export-lst/02-populated-fields-reference.md). Today Unload Sequence is populated purely from pile membership order; this use case requires it to additionally reflect a safety/productivity-ranked sequence, computed across piles, not just within one pile.

Multi-part-pick optimization

Where the automation can pick more than one part per cycle (see 01-automation-types/), the sequencing strategy should:

  • Group parts that can be placed in parallel by the same pick so they are scheduled together, rather than being spread across unrelated unload cycles.
  • Keep piles belonging to a related/simultaneous pick group close together on the pallet, minimizing gripper travel distance and cycle time — this feeds back into pile placement (Auto Arrange proximity objective), even though the driving concern here is pick-cycle count, not area usage.

Relationship to existing chapters

  • Builds on the pile membership/order model in 05-stack-and-pile-management/01-stack-data-model.md and the Unload Sequence field it produces.
  • Is a further candidate objective for the pluggable scoring function raised in 05-auto-suggested-stacking-patterns.md, alongside minimal area and order grouping.
  • Must still respect reachability (02-reachability-and-non-reachable-zones.md) and collision constraints (04-collision-hazard-of-repositioned-parts.md) — sequencing optimizes within the set of placements those chapters already allow, it does not override them.

Open point

The relative priority between the objectives named here (idle-time reduction, sheet safety, gripper economy, travel minimization) and the objectives from 05-auto-suggested-stacking-patterns.md (minimal area, order grouping) is not defined by the source material and is left as a pluggable, ranked objective set rather than a fixed formula.
