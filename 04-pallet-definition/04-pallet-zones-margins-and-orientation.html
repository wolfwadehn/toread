Pallet Zones, Margins, and Orientation

Margins

Directly inherited from the Deposit Editor's Deposit Settings dialog: margins from each edge of the pallet, with parts deposited starting from the lower left corner (or the configured ReferenceCorner — see 01-pallet-data-model.md); if there is not enough space, parts overflow beyond the far edge (single pallet) or onto the next pallet in a multi-pallet layout (see 03-custom-pallets-and-multi-pallet-layout.md). Full behavior detailed in 05-stack-and-pile-management/06-deposit-settings.md.

Reachability zone vs. margin

Margins are a placement rule (keep piles off the physical edge); the reachable/non-reachable zone (see 03-gripper-definition/02-gripper-geometry-and-reach-envelope.md) is a feasibility rule (can the gripper get there at all). The concept paper's pallet diagram shows this concretely: the top strip of the pallet (nearest the machine, marked in red/pink) is a non-reachable zone regardless of margin settings, distinct from the configured margin band.

Orientation zones

The concept paper's example ties pallet zoning to orientation capability: a part that can only be nested/cut at 0° (because its automation cannot rotate it, see 01-automation-types/03-sheetmaster-and-sortmaster.md) may be restricted to "the lower half of the pallet." This is modeled as an orientation-restricted zone: a sub-region of the pallet footprint that only accepts piles whose parts share one fixed in-plane angle.

Proposed fields (extends Pallet Data Model)

Field Type Description
Zones list Named sub-regions, each with an outline polygon.
Zones[].AllowedAngles list of angle | Any Restricts which part orientations may be placed in this zone.
Zones[].ReachOverride bool Whether this zone overrides the automation's default reach envelope (e.g. a zone known in advance to be non-reachable, independent of gripper geometry).

See 06-process-and-collision-constraints/03-orientation-constraints-from-downstream-process.md for how orientation zones are enforced during stacking.

Overhang allowance [Target]

Margins keep piles inside the pallet edge. Overhang is the opposite case: a part larger than the load carrier must be palletized anyway, with an explicitly allowed overhang beyond the pallet footprint on one or more edges. Where a margin is a maximum-inward constraint, an overhang allowance is a maximum-outward tolerance — the two are not the same field and must not be merged: a pallet can have both a margin (for normally-sized parts) and an overhang allowance (for oversized parts placed deliberately past the edge). Overhang must be validated against reachability (06-process-and-collision-constraints/02-reachability-and-non-reachable-zones.md) and collision checks separately, since an overhanging part changes the stack's effective footprint for the unloader and for downstream equipment.
