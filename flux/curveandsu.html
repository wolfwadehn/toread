Curve and Surface Parametrization

Curve and Surface Parametrization

V1 ● 20150228 ● AV

Curves

Line3

A line is defined in its own coordinate system and stretches along the X axis in that system. Technically, the orientations of the Y and Z axes are irrelevant, so only the origin and X-vector are stored rather than a complete coordinate system.

Definition: The parametrization is in mm and is simply the distance along the X axis.

Arc3

An arc is defined in its own local coordinate system, where it lies in the XY plane and winds around the Z axis. The center of the arc is at the origin, and T=0 corresponds to a point lying on the X axis. T is measured in radians.

Definition: R is the radius of the arc.

Ellipse3

The parametrization of Ellipse3 is identical to that of an arc. The ellipse is defined in the XY plane with center at the origin. T is measured in radians, winding clockwise about the X axis.

image:image19.png[image19,width=174,height=119]

image:image20.png[image20,width=226,height=159]

Definition: XRadius is the radius measured along the X axis. YRadius is the radius measured along the Y axis.

Note that the X and Y directions must be the major and minor axes of the ellipse, though either of them can be the major and the other the minor. That is, XRadius may be smaller or larger than YRadius.

ConicCurve3

The parametrization of ConicCurve3 varies depending on the form of the curve. This type can represent ellipses, hyperbolas, or parabolas, all the conic sections, and the parametrization is different for each type. For an exhaustive definition, see the IGES reference, Entity type 104.

This is rarely used except to import type 104 entities from IGES. It is possible that in the future this may be deprecated and converted into NurbCurve on import.

PiecewiseCubic3

This represents a piecewise cubic curve. There are multiple knots in the curve, and between each knot the curve is a cubic with its own cubic polynomial coefficients. The parametrization can be understood by studying the polynomial curve, Entity type 112, in the IGES reference.

Like ConicCurve3, this may be deprecated in the future and converted into NurbCurve on import.

Polyline3

This represents a simple piecewise linear approximation given by N points where N >= 2. The parametrization goes from 0 .. N-1.

The integer part of the parameter is used to select a segment within the polyline, and the fractional part is the lie along that segment, where 0 is start of segment and 1 is end of segment. Thus, a point with parameter 2.5 lies at the midpoint of the third segment in the Polyline3.

image:image21.png[image21,width=293,height=108]

NurbCurve3

This is a classic NURBS curve, and the parametrization is given by the knot vector.

Surfaces

Cylinder

A cylinder is defined in its own local coordinate space and is then lofted into the desired location and orientation using a transform.

Definition: R is the radius of the cylinder in mm.

Parametrization: U goes around the Z axis, along the XY plane, and is in radians from 0 .. 2PI. V goes along the Z axis, is in mm, and is unbounded.

Cone

A cone is defined in its own local coordinate space and has a transform to move it into the desired location and orientation.

Definition: R is the radius of the cone at the XY plane. As we go higher in Z, the radius increases. A is the semi-angle subtended by the cone at the tip. At a given Z, radius is R + tan(A).

Parametrization: U goes around the Z axis, along the XY plane, and is in radians from 0 .. 2PI. V goes vertically along the Z axis and is in mm.

image:image22.png[image22,width=196,height=164]

image:image23.png[image23,width=206,height=205]

Plane

A plane is defined in its own local coordinate system. In this coordinate system, the plane coincides with the XY plane.

Parametrization: U is in mm along the X axis of the local coordinate system, from -Infinity .. +Infinity. V is in mm along the Y axis of the local coordinate system, from -Infinity .. +Infinity.

RuledSurface

A RuledSurface is defined by two generator curves. The surface is obtained by connecting corresponding points of these two curves with straight lines. The two generator curves must therefore have equal parametric domains.

Definition: Cb is the bottom generator curve. Ct is the top generator curve, which must have the same parameter domain as Cb.

Parametrization: V is used to evaluate points Pb on curve Cb and Pt on curve Ct, using V as parameter input to these curves. V must lie between Tmin .. Tmax of the domain of Cb. U is used to interpolate between Pb and Pt and must lie between 0 .. 1.

Sphere

image:image24.png[image24,width=140,height=117]

image:image25.png[image25,width=234,height=150]

A sphere is defined in its own local coordinate system.

Definition: R is the radius of the sphere in mm.

Parametrization: U goes around the Z axis, along the XY plane, and is in radians from 0 .. 2PI. V goes vertically and is the elevation angle of the point from the XY plane. The domain is -PI .. +PI.

SpunSurface

A SpunSurface is defined by rotating a generator curve about the Z axis.

Definition: Cg is the parametric generator curve that is spun around the Z axis.

Parametrization: V is used to evaluate a point Peval on the generator curve Cg. The domain of V is the same as the T domain of Cg. U is used to rotate point Peval around the Z axis in a counter-clockwise direction and is measured in radians. The domain of U is 0 .. 2PI.

Note: The picture often shows a generator curve lying in the XZ plane, but this is not mandatory. The generator curve may be positioned anywhere in space and need not be planar. The point obtained at U=0 is not necessarily in the XZ plane. Wherever that point is, it is rotated about the Z axis by U radians to get the final point.

SweptSurface

image:image26.png[image26,width=191,height=163]

image:image27.png[image27,width=196,height=204]

A SweptSurface is defined by moving a generator curve Cg along a direction D.

Definition: Cg is the parametric generator curve that is swept. Dir is a unit vector defining the sweep direction.

Parametrization: U is used to evaluate a point Peval on the generator curve Cg. The domain of U is the same as the T domain of Cg. V is used as a distance to move Peval along direction vector Dir. V is measured in mm, and the domain of V is -Infinity .. +Infinity.

Torus

A torus is defined in its local coordinate system and is lofted into space with a transformation. A torus is defined by taking a small circle with center on the X axis, lying in the XZ plane, and sweeping it around the Z axis.

Definition: RMajor is the radius of the big circle traced by the centerline of the torus tube. RMinor is the radius of the small circle rotated around to form the torus.

Parametrization: U is the rotation of the generator circle about the Z axis. U=0 corresponds to the generator circle lying in the XY plane. U goes from 0 .. 2PI. V is the angle around the generator circle itself. At U=0, V=0 corresponds to a point at RMajor + RMinor, the furthest point. As V increases, we go around the generator circle counter-clockwise.
